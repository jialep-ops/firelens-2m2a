What is in this prototype?

Eleven curated source records and 646 retained NASA PSI test rows from NASA PSI investigation metadata, NTRS reports, and NASA Science pages. Records summarize findings or objectives; raw videos and detector-response time series have not been ingested. Seven original experimental CSV tables are available in the Research desk, with DOI citations, file hashes, preserved raw cells, and documented row exclusions.

How ranking works

Local BM25 text retrieval over titles, summaries, implications, and topic tags. Common words are removed; simple suffix normalization and a small synonym dictionary improve matching. Scores indicate text relevance, never hazard, confidence, or scientific quality.

AI transparency

ChatGPT assisted research, coding, and drafting. The app ranks source summaries with deterministic text retrieval. Its server can generate citation-constrained explanations when a VT ARC or OpenAI model connection is configured on the server. Without that connection, it explicitly uses curated evidence mode. Citation IDs and numeric support are checked against the supplied source pack; this does not prove factual entailment. It does not provide an autonomous safety assessment.

Evidence boundaries

Observed findings, experiment designs, research objectives, and explainers are explicitly labeled. Interpretive “Why it matters” text is a prototype synthesis, not a NASA operational recommendation.

Microgravity findings do not automatically transfer to lunar gravity, Martian gravity, new fuels, or new cabin conditions. Always consult the full source.

Next research steps

Next: expert review of the source summaries and retrieval, broader PSI coverage, detector time-series analysis, and quantitative uncertainty where NASA reports it. The participant supplied the full challenge Resources list, which explicitly names PSI, the source of our seven datasets. The source match is confirmed against that supplied text.
